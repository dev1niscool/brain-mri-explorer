How this view works

The renderer samples through the MRI along each line of sight. Translucent blends the samples, X-ray adds their signal, and Max intensity picks the brightest. “X-ray” names a visual effect on MRI data; it is not an X-ray acquisition or a skull-bone segmentation.

Whole head shows the captured field of view. Brain only keeps the MRI inside the HD-BET model’s approximate boundary, including its filled internal spaces. The display uses a 1.25 mm interpolated grid and normalized brightness values. Inspired by Max Allison’s MRI viewer ↗; built here with Devin’s own scan.

Simplify the mesh for your slicer.

  1. Try the full STL first. Triangle count controls mesh complexity. It does not set your printer’s layer height.
  2. For a lighter mesh, use Compact. It is already simplified and checked for closed edges.
  3. To simplify it yourself in PrusaSlicer: right-click the model → Simplify model. Adjust the detail level or triangle reduction, turn on Show wireframe, and inspect the preview before applying.
  4. Check the sliced layers. Review the folds, thin areas and supports after setting your print size. A smooth screen preview does not guarantee a successful physical print.

Methods & sources

Lengths are model bounds along RAS axes (right, anterior, superior). Enclosed volume comes from the closed triangle surface. Dimensions are rounded for display; these values are not clinically validated anatomical measurements.

Head outlines are the largest closed loops where horizontal planes intersect the model. Length and width are the longer and shorter sides of each outline’s minimum-area enclosing rectangle. The circle has the same enclosed area. Neither the circle nor the rectangle is a population norm.

The head model includes scalp and face. Its lower field of view is cropped and closed. Brain and head came from separate acquisitions and have not been motion-registered. No brain-to-head occupancy ratio is calculated.

Privacy & consent

Identifying DICOM metadata has been removed from the derived dataset. Devin explicitly chose to publish the full head and face. Facial anatomy can be recognizable: this collection is not anonymous.

What you’re seeing

Real MRI images and automatically reconstructed surfaces. Pink is an illustrative display color; MRI does not capture tissue color. Brain2Print’s colored labels are automated estimates of tissues and structures; they do not trace individual lobes. No diagnosis or clinical interpretation is provided.

Resolution matters

The anatomical scans use 5 mm slices separated by 6.5 mm center to center. Smooth 3D views interpolate across the gaps. Interpolation does not create new anatomical detail.

Head, brain, and bone

The outer model represents the head surface. Conventional MRI does not reliably separate skull bone. The brain surface is an automated approximation and has not been reviewed for clinical use.
